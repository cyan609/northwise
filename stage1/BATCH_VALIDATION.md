### Pattern: unnumbered pages only ever end a chapter

In every chapter that has unnumbered pages, those pages form a **contiguous
run ending at the chapter's last page**. No chapter has an unnumbered page
in the middle followed by numbered pages.

This matters for interpretation: it is consistent with the final page(s) of
a chapter being unnumbered by design, rather than with a folio that the
extractor intermittently fails to read. It also means the risk is
concentrated at chapter ends, where a citation is least likely to land.

## What was and was not checked

- **Automated, every chapter:** record count vs PDF page count; folio
  status distribution; duplicate labels; offset consistency; numeric gaps.
- **Manual, a small subset:** see `STAGE1_INVENTORY.md`. The great majority
  of records are machine-extracted only and are not claimed to be manually
  verified.

## Limitations

1. `verified` means a folio was read from the footer, not that a human
   compared the page. Only the pages listed in the inventory were inspected
   visually.
2. A folio is still not a unique page identifier where duplicates exist;
   citations must carry `pdf_page_index`.
3. Text order comes from PDFKit, so table cells can interleave with prose on
   dense pages. This does not affect folio detection but will affect
   retrieval quality and must be measured before Q&A.
4. The chapter files are English-only as published; there is no Chinese
   chapter text to extract.
